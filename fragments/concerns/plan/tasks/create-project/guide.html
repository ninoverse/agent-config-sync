The header

Title
The main repository and the project's objective, joined by a dash, such as example-service-health-checks, and under it what the project is for.
Calendar
The month the statuses were read in, that day outlined; the arrows step through the project's other months. Each day lists its events, outlined, the days phases start, end or reopen, and its items, counted by what happened to them in the board's colours, such as 18 merged. A day with something on it opens the Agenda there.
PR statuses as of
When the PRs were last read from GitHub: anything later isn't on the page yet.
Who holds the ball
The owner: what waits on the person who decides, merges and changes settings, each with what to do, such as Merge or Decide, the Plan first until they approve it, and how many items wait for them to say. The agent: the items that are Ready. A date: the next three, each a link to its day in the Agenda; Automated marks a run that starts on its own.
Agenda, Guides, Warnings
Top right, each over the page; its close button, or Escape, brings you back. Warnings shows only when building the page found something the data may have wrong, with how many, each under the file to fix.

The phases

A tab per phase, in two groups: Phases, the six numbered in the order they run, and What it leaves: Reuse, the Retrospective and Deferred. A phase starts once those before it are done; one that starts early says why, in the Agenda. Verify, Reuse and the Retrospective end only after Build, and Build and Verify reopen when new work comes in. Under each name is the phase's state:

  • Done, or Amended once items came in after it ended or was approved: each is marked Amended, with its day and why, and the phase lists them at its end.
  • Now, with how many of its items are done, such as 3/6, not counting dropped or set-aside ones. Deferred says instead how many items wait.
  • To come, or Not needed. A tab with a dashed border doesn't open: its phase is Not needed, or nothing is written in it yet.

A phase other than the one in progress loads the first time it opens; if it can't, it says so, with a button to try again.

1 Brief
The problem, the objective and the scope; the success criteria (SC1…); the ground rules (G1…), how the owner wants the project run; and the repositories, each with what the project does with it:
2 Research
What exists, one piece at a time (R1…), and what each raises.
3 Analysis
The decisions (D1…), each with its options, the one recommended and why, and the owner's answer. The evidence behind them is in the Research.
4 Plan
The PRs, the settings only the owner can change (S1…) and the risks (RK1…), as the owner approved them. It ends with its approval, and the items added since.
5 Build
The board, what each item's merge or change brought once it lands, and the questions the work raises (Q1…).
6 Verify
The checks (C1…): those of a branch, which its PR waits on before it merges, and those that prove each merged PR does what its Done when says; and the success criteria, Met or Not met yet.
Reuse
What another project can take from this one, a card each. Often Not needed.
Retrospective
The project's record, built from the other phases: how each phase went, the roads not taken, the risks, what it took, what comes next and every decision; the lessons (L1…) it taught; and, for a project shown outside it, its story, in the owner's voice.
Deferred
What is set aside (T1…), here or in another phase, each with what it waits on, or where it goes.

Number and summary
The name the item goes by, and a link to its card from anywhere on the page. The letters say what it is: G ground rule, SC success criterion, R research piece, D decision, Q question, S setting, RK risk, C check, L lesson, T deferred work; a PR takes its repository's prefix, such as AC-1. A number is never reused.
Chips
The kind; the status, in the item's own words, with its day; Amended, with the day it came in; and the link to where it lives, such as its PR. A card shows its status only in the phase that keeps it: the Plan shows its PRs as approved, and Build where they stand.
Options
A decision's choices: the recommended one is marked until the owner chooses, then only the one chosen.
Needs, Needed by, Carried out by, Mentioned in
The items around it, each a link, so a decision leads to the PRs that carry it out, and on to the checks that prove them. A criterion is Verified by its checks, and a risk Closes with its step.

Statuses

Each status takes its item's words: a decision is Taken, a PR Merged, a risk Closed.

  • Waits on the owner: the owner acts next, as the header lists.
  • Ready: everything it needs is done, and the agent can start it.
  • Blocked: it waits on something, named beside its chip.
  • Done, with the day. A risk that came true is Happened instead, naming what took it over.
  • Dropped: struck through, with why. An item is never deleted: one that no longer belongs is Dropped.
  • Deferred: set aside until a trigger it names, or until the owner says, which Your go marks, or Your call on a question or a decision. To be moved: another project will take it. Moved: one has, and a chip links its card there. Deferred lists the first two, and none of the three holds its phase open.

Each type in its words, and the owner's part while one waits on them:

Kinds

The first chip on a card, and the last word on a board chip. A PR's kind is what its merge releases; Owner marks what only the owner can do, such as a setting.

The board

The order of the work, in Build. Columns are waves: a wave starts once what its items need is done, and its subtitle says what it waits for. Rows are repositories, each with one PR open at a time. A board wider than the screen opens at its first column with work left.

A done chip fades; a blocked one is dashed, and says what it waits on; a deferred one is grey and dashed, with what it waits on, or Your go; and a dropped one is struck through. A chip opens its card.

The Agenda

Coming up
Every date still ahead, soonest first.
Notes for the next session
What is in flight and what comes next, as the last session left them.
What happened
Every day before, newest first, with the calendar's entries, each count a list of its items, such as Merged over the PRs that merged that day. A line marks the day the statuses were read.

Answering

The page only shows the state: nothing on it changes anything. The owner answers in the chat with the agent, naming items by number:

  • D3: b, to take option b of a decision;
  • T2: go, to start an item deferred until the owner says;
  • S1 is set, once the setting is changed.

A PR needs no message: the agent reads each one from GitHub before it publishes. It updates the project's data, publishes the page again, and says in the chat what changed.
