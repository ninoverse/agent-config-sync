The header

The top of every view: what the project is, where it stands, and the way to everything else.

Title
The main repository and the project's objective, joined by a dash, such as example-service-health-checks. The line under it says what the project is for.
Tabs

One per phase, numbered in the order the phases run; 0 is this guide. The highlighted tab is the one you're reading. Under each name is the phase's state:

  • Done: the phase is over, and its view is the record of it.
  • Now: in progress. Its tab also counts the items it tracks that are done, such as 3/6; dropped items aren't counted. Two phases can be Now at once: Verify starts with the first merge, while Build goes on.
  • To come: not started yet.
  • Not needed: skipped. Its tab has a dashed border, and its view says why.
Calendar
Opens the Calendar, described below. Beside it, at a glance: how many items wait on you, how many are next for the agent, and the next date coming up.
State read on
When the statuses were last checked against GitHub and the repositories. Anything that happened after that isn't on the page yet.

The phases

Each phase has a view of its own. Its head shows its state and its dates: since 5 Oct while it runs, 1 Oct – 2 Oct once it's done.

1 Brief
The problem, the objective, what is in scope and what is out, the success criteria (SC1, SC2…), the repositories involved, and the ground rules (G1, G2…): how you want the project run. A ground rule holds until you change it.
2 Research
What exists today, one numbered piece at a time (R1, R2…), and what each piece raises: usually a decision for the Analysis.
3 Analysis
The decisions (D1, D2…), each with its options, a recommendation and its reasons, and your answer with its date. When the decisions are many, they come in batches, each a section with its evidence and what it costs.
4 Plan
The PRs as you approved them, one at a time in each repository; the settings only you can change (S1…); and the risk register (RK1…), each risk with the step that closes it. Its first lines say when you approved it, and list anything added since, each with its date and why.
5 Build
Where everything stands: the board, the results, the questions the work raises (Q1…), and the log.
6 Verify
The checks (C1…) that prove each PR does what its Done when says, where it runs, and the Brief's success criteria, Met or Not met yet. Build ends with the last merge; Verify ends with the last check.
7 Reuse
What other projects can take from this one: a template, a shared rule or workflow, or a page to link instead. Often Not needed, with why.
8 Retrospective
The project told for a portfolio, in your voice: the problem, the approach, the outcome, the figures, counted from the data where they can be, and the lessons (L1…) it taught.
9 For later
What was deferred (T1…), what each item waits on, and where it goes when that comes. Deferring an item here lets the Retrospective go ahead without it.

Sections

A phase is a stack of sections, each with a title, and often a grey line under it saying what it holds. They come in a few kinds.

Cards
One card per item: a decision, a PR, a setting, a check. The next part of this guide takes one apart.
Tables
Facts in rows, such as the repositories involved. On a narrow screen, a wide table scrolls sideways in its own box, while the page stays put.
Board
The order of the work, in waves. It has a part of this guide to itself.
Results
One row per item: its status, the day it got there, and what came of it, such as the version a merge released or what a check showed.
Log
What happened, by day, newest first.
Figures
Headline numbers. The note under them says which were counted from the project's data, and on what day.
Definitions
A term and what it means here, such as the Brief's Problem and Objective.
Imported pages
A page written before this one, kept whole inside a phase, such as an earlier version of the Analysis.
Changelog
At the foot of every phase: what changed in it, and when.

First line
The item's number and its summary: the name it goes by wherever it's linked. The number never changes and is never reused, and its letters say what the item is: G ground rule, SC success criterion, R research piece, D decision, Q question, S setting, RK risk, C check, L lesson, T later work. A PR carries its repository's prefix, such as AC-1.
Chips
What a word or two says: the kind, described under Kinds; the status, in the item's own words, with the day it got there; Amended, with the day the item was added after its phase was approved; and the link to where the item lives, such as its PR on GitHub. A card shows its status only in the phase that keeps it: the Plan shows its PRs as approved, and Build says where they stand; the Brief shows its success criteria, and Verify says whether they're met.
Title
The full title, when the summary is shorter. A PR's is the title it merges with.
Options
A decision's options, as a list. Chips mark the one recommended and the one you chose, and the chosen one is highlighted. The reasons for the recommendation follow.
Fields
The longer facts, each a label and its text, such as Branch, Changes, or Done when: what proves a PR works.
Waits on, Why
What a blocked item waits on, or, for a risk, the step it closes with; and why a dropped one was dropped.
Needs
What has to be done first. The board's waves come from it.
Why amended
Why the item was added after its phase was approved.
Needed by, Named by
The items that need this one, and the items that mention it: a decision's card lists the PRs that carry it out.

Statuses

Every item is in one of five states, and the page words each one the way the item's type says it: a decision is Taken, a PR Merged, a risk Closed.

  • Waits on you: you act next, such as merging a PR, taking a decision or changing a setting. The Calendar lists these under Waiting on you, each with what to do.
  • Ready: everything it needs is done, and the agent can start it.
  • Blocked: it waits on something, which the page names: waits on an item or an event, or, for a risk, closes with the step that closes it.
  • Done: finished, with the day.
  • Dropped: struck through, with why.

Each row below is a type of item, in the words its statuses take, and what you do while one waits on you:

Kinds

The first chip on a card, and the word at the end of a chip on the board, say what kind of change an item is.

The board

The order of the work, in Build.

  • Columns are waves. The items in one wave can be under way at the same time; a wave starts once what its items need is done. Its subtitle says what it waits for.
  • Rows are repositories. Each has at most one PR open at a time. A setting sits in the row of the repository it configures.
  • Each chip is an item: its number and short name, then its status in words, the day it got there, and its kind.

A chip's stripe and tint follow its status, so a glance at the board says where the work stands:

A done chip fades, a blocked one has a dashed border and says what it waits on, and a dropped one is struck through, with why. Click a chip to open its card.

The Calendar

The Calendar button in the header opens a view of everything with a date, and of who acts next.

Who holds the ball
Three columns. Waiting on you: every item that waits on you, each with what to do, such as Merge or Decide. Next for me: what the agent does next, the items that are Ready. Waiting on a date: the next dates coming up.
Notes for the next session
What is in flight, where, and what comes next, as the last session left them. A new session reads them before anything else.
The calendar
The months the project spans, today outlined. Each day lists what happened or is coming: events, status changes in their status's colours, amendments, and the days phases start and end. A dot is a line of the log; point at it to read it. On a phone, every entry is a dot.
Every date
The same entries as a list, newest first. Dates still ahead are in bold, and a line marks today.

Links and moving around

  • A number with its name, such as D1 Where the probes live, is a link to that item's card. It opens the card even when another phase holds it.
  • The tabs switch phases, and the Calendar button opens the Calendar; a phase opens where you are, without scrolling.
  • A phase other than the one in progress loads the first time you open it. If it can't, the page says so, with a button to try again.
  • On a card, Needs, Needed by and Named by link to the items around it, so you can follow a decision to the PRs that carry it out, and on to the checks that prove them.

What changed, and when

  • Each phase ends with its changelog, newest first: what changed on each day, with links to the sections and items it changed. A correction is an entry of its own, saying what was wrong.
  • The header says when the state was last read. Anything later isn't on the page yet.
  • The Calendar's list of every date holds all of it by day, from the project's first date to its last.

Answering

The page only shows the state: nothing on it changes anything. Answer in your chat with the agent, naming items by number:

  • D3: b, to take option b of a decision;
  • EX-2 is merged, once you've merged a PR;
  • S1 is set, once you've changed a setting.

The agent updates the project's data and publishes the page again, and the changelog of each phase it touched says what changed.
