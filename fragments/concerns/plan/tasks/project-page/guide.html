The header

The top of every view: what the project is, its calendar, where it stands, and the way to everything else.

Agenda, Guides
Top right, the two views that aren't phases: the Agenda, described below, and these guides, with the glossary. Each opens over the whole page; its close button, or Escape, brings you back to the phase you were reading.
Title
The main repository and the project's objective, joined by a dash, such as example-service-health-checks. The line under it says what the project is for.
Calendar
The month the statuses were read in, that day outlined; the arrows step through the other months the project spans. Each day lists what happened or is coming: its events, outlined; the days phases start and end, a phase done within a day as one, and an approval in place of the end it marks; then its items, counted by what happened to them in the board's colours, such as 18 merged or 2 amended. On a phone, each is a box, and a count's box shows its number. A day with something on it opens the Agenda at that day.
PR statuses as of
Under the calendar: when the PRs were last read from GitHub, so anything that happened after isn't on the page yet.
Who holds the ball
Three blocks, one under another, one for each that can hold it. The owner: every item that waits on the person who decides, merges and changes settings, each with what to do, such as Merge or Decide, and under them how many items wait on them but not yet, deferred until they say, with a link to Deferred. The agent: what the agent does next, the items that are Ready. A date: the next three dates, each a link to its day in the Agenda, which lists the rest; Automated marks a run that starts on its own, such as a scheduled workflow.
Phases, What it leaves

A tab per phase, in two groups: Phases, the six numbered in the order they run, and What it leaves, the three that don't run in that order: Reuse, the Retrospective and Deferred. The highlighted tab is the one you're reading. Under each name is the phase's state:

  • Done: the phase is over, and its view is the record of it.
  • Now: in progress. Its tab also counts the items it tracks that are done, such as 3/6; dropped items aren't counted. Two phases can be Now at once: Verify starts with the first merge, while Build goes on.
  • To come: not started yet.
  • Not needed: skipped.

A phase starts once those before it are done; one that starts early says why, on its day in the Agenda. Verify, Reuse and the Retrospective end only after Build, and Build and Verify reopen when new work comes in. Deferred's tab says instead how many items wait, such as 15 waiting. A tab with a dashed border doesn't open: its phase is Not needed, or nothing is written in it yet.

The phases

Each phase has a view of its own, which its tab opens once something is written in it. The tab shows the phase's state, and the calendar the days it started and ended. The first six run in their order; the last three are what the project leaves.

1 Brief
The problem, the objective, what is in scope and what is out, the success criteria (SC1, SC2…), each with the checks that verify it, the repositories involved, each with what the project does with it, and the ground rules (G1, G2…): how the owner wants the project run. A ground rule holds until the owner changes it.
2 Research
What exists today, one numbered piece at a time (R1, R2…), and what each piece raises: usually a decision for the Analysis.
3 Analysis
The decisions (D1, D2…), each with its options, a recommendation and its reasons, and your answer with its date. When the decisions are many, they come in batches, each a section; the evidence behind them is in the Research.
4 Plan
The PRs as the owner approved them, one at a time in each repository; the settings only the owner can change (S1…); and the risk register (RK1…), each risk with the step that closes it. It ends with its approval: the day the owner approved it, and the items added since, grouped by the day they came in and why.
5 Build
Where everything stands: the board, the results, and the questions the work raises (Q1…).
6 Verify
The checks (C1…), each titled by how it is checked, that prove each PR does what its Done when says, where it runs, and the Brief's success criteria, Met or Not met yet. Build ends with the last merge; Verify ends with the last check.
Reuse
What other projects can take from this one: a template, a shared rule or workflow, or a page to link instead, each with how to take it and the items that built it. Often Not needed.
Retrospective
The project told for a portfolio, in your voice: the problem, the approach, the outcome, the figures, counted from the data where they can be, and the lessons (L1…) it taught.
Deferred
What was deferred (T1…), and what other phases have deferred or are to move to another project, each with what it waits on and where it goes. Deferring an item lets the Retrospective go ahead without it.

Sections

A phase is a stack of sections, each with a title. They come in a few kinds.

Cards
One card per item: a decision, a PR, a setting, a check. The next part of this guide takes one apart.
Tables
Facts in rows. On a narrow screen, a wide table scrolls sideways in its own box, while the page stays put, and a shade on an edge says more of the table lies past it.
Repositories
The Brief's table of the repositories the project involves, each with a chip for what the project does with it:
Board
The order of the work, in waves. It has a part of this guide to itself.
Results
A card per item whose status the phase keeps: the status, the day it got there, and what the item waits on, or what came of it, such as the version a merge released. An item the board shows is here only once it has a result, and then with the result alone, since the board says the rest, or once it has moved, with the link to its new page. Each card folds, as an item's does.
Figures
Headline numbers. The note under them says which were counted from the project's data, and on what day.
Definitions
A card of terms, each with what it means here, such as the Brief's Problem and Objective. In Reuse, each term is a card of its own, titled by it: a piece another project can take.
Contents
At the top of the Analysis and the Plan, when they have several sections: each section, a link to its cards, and the Plan's approval.
Imported pages
A page written before this one, kept whole inside a phase, such as an earlier version of the Analysis.
Approval
At the end of a phase the owner approves, such as the Plan: the day they did, and the items added since.

Cards

Every item is a card, in three layers: what it is, a line of chips, then the longer facts. On the page, a card shows the first two, and a click opens the rest; a link to a card opens it, and Open all, above a section's cards, opens them all. These three samples are open, to show the parts a card can have, described after them; most cards have fewer.

First line
The item's number and its summary: the name it goes by wherever it's linked. The number never changes and is never reused, and its letters say what the item is: G ground rule, SC success criterion, R research piece, D decision, Q question, S setting, RK risk, C check, L lesson, T deferred work. A PR carries its repository's prefix, such as AC-1.
Chips
What a word or two says: the kind, described under Kinds; the status, in the item's own words, with the day it got there; Amended, with the day the item was added after its phase was approved; and the link to where the item lives, such as its PR on GitHub. A project can add chips of its own, such as what a comparison found. A card shows its status only in the phase that keeps it: the Plan shows its PRs as approved, and Build says where they stand; the Brief shows its success criteria, To agree until the owner agrees them, or Dropped, and Verify says whether they're met.
Title
The full title, when the summary is shorter. A PR's is the title it merges with.
Options
A decision's options, as a list. Until the owner chooses, the recommended one is grey; once they have, only the one chosen is marked, in green. The reasons for the recommendation follow.
Fields
The longer facts, each a label and its text, such as Branch, Changes, or Done when: what proves a PR works. A field that holds several things, such as the decisions a PR carries out, is a list.
Waits on, Why
What a blocked item waits on, or, for a risk, the step it closes with; and why a dropped one was dropped.
Needs
What has to be done first. The board's waves come from it. On a success criterion, it reads Verified by: the checks that prove the criterion is met; on a risk, Closes with: the step that closes it.
Why amended
Why the item was added after its phase was approved.
Needed by, Carried out by, Mentioned in
The items that need this one, and the items that mention it, each a list. A decision's card lists the PRs and settings that name it as Carried out by, and anything else as Mentioned in. On a check, the criteria that need it read Verifies; on a step, the risks it closes read Closes.

Statuses

Every item is in one of five states, a risk can end in a sixth, and any item can be set aside in three more; the page words each one the way the item's type says it: a decision is Taken, a PR Merged, a risk Closed.

  • Waits on the owner: the owner acts next, such as merging a PR, taking a decision or changing a setting. The header lists these under The owner, each with what to do.
  • Ready: everything it needs is done, and the agent can start it.
  • Blocked: it waits on something, which the page names beside its chip, even on a folded card: waits on an item or an event, or, for a risk, closes with the step that closes it.
  • Done: finished, with the day.
  • Happened: a risk only, when what it describes came true, with the day and the question or the blocker that took it over.
  • Dropped: struck through, with why.
  • Deferred: any item, parked until a trigger outside the project, which the page names beside its chip, or until the owner says, which Your go beside it marks, or Your call on a question or a decision. Deferred lists it, and it no longer holds its phase open.
  • To be moved: any item another project will take, named beside its chip. Deferred lists it too.
  • Moved: any item another project has taken, with a chip to its card on that project's page, where a chip links back. It is done with here.

Each row below is a type of item, in the words its statuses take, and what the owner does while one waits on them:

Kinds

The first chip on a card, and the word at the end of a chip on the board, say what kind of change an item is. A PR's kind is what its merge releases, so no field repeats it.

The board

The order of the work, in Build.

  • Columns are waves. The items in one wave can be under way at the same time; a wave starts once what its items need is done. Its subtitle says what it waits for.
  • Rows are repositories. Each has at most one PR open at a time. A setting sits in the row of the repository it configures.
  • Each chip is an item: its number and short name, then its status in words, the day it got there, and its kind.
  • It opens at the work left. A board wider than the screen opens at its first column with an unfinished item, the finished ones scrolled past to its left, and the rows' names stay in view as it scrolls.

A chip's stripe and tint follow its status, so a glance at the board says where the work stands:

A done chip fades, a blocked one has a dashed border and says what it waits on, and a dropped one is struck through, with why. Click a chip to open its card.

The Agenda

The Agenda button, top right, opens over the page everything with a date, the ones still ahead first. Who holds the ball is in the header, on every view.

Coming up
Every date still ahead, soonest first.
Notes for the next session
What is in flight, where, and what comes next, as the last session left them. A new session reads them before anything else.
What happened
Every date before, newest first, back to the project's first: the same entries as the calendar, each count a list of its items under what happened to them, such as Merged over the PRs that merged that day. A line marks the day the statuses were read.

Links and moving around

  • A number with its name, such as D1 Where the probes live, is a link to that item's card. It opens the card even when another phase holds it.
  • The tabs switch phases, and a phase opens where you are, without scrolling. The buttons top right open the Agenda and the Guides over the page, and their close button, or Escape, closes them. A day in the calendar opens the Agenda at that day.
  • A phase other than the one in progress loads the first time you open it. If it can't, the page says so, with a button to try again.
  • On a card, Needs, Needed by, Carried out by and Mentioned in link to the items around it, so you can follow a decision to the PRs that carry it out, and on to the checks that prove them.

What changed, and when

  • The Agenda holds it all by day: what is coming up, soonest first, then what happened, newest first.
  • An item is never deleted. One that no longer belongs is Dropped, saying why, and the Agenda has the day.
  • Under the calendar, the header says when the PR statuses were last read. Anything later isn't on the page yet.

Answering

The page only shows the state: nothing on it changes anything. The owner answers in the chat with the agent, naming items by number:

  • D3: b, to take option b of a decision;
  • EX-2 is merged, once the PR is merged;
  • S1 is set, once the setting is changed.

The agent updates the project's data, publishes the page again, and says in the chat what changed.
